7. Release 1 Prototype and Demonstration

The Release 1 prototype must show, on realistic sample data, that MCMS handles the whole life of a micro-credential and keeps each recognition stage separate. This section fixes the demonstration scenario, the seed data and the behaviour of the stub adapters, so that the Release 1 prototype and its demonstration can be built and tested against a known script. This scenario is the acceptance script of Release 1 (D-31): the UI/UX Design Specification flow FL-19 and the Playwright end-to-end test follow it, act by act.

7.1 Demonstration Scenario

The scenario follows three offerings — one of each category — and one participant, Amir, through every stage. It is the script for the supervisor demonstration (D-23) and the end-to-end acceptance test of Release 1.

8. Experience and Design Principles

8.1 Design System

All screens follow the project design system DESIGN.md "ulearn-carbon": IBM Carbon's flat, square geometry; IBM Plex Sans; white and light-gray surfaces; charcoal text; and uLearn Navy (#1e2a6e) as the single accent for links, primary actions and focus. MCMS adds the product rules below on top of it.

8.2 Product Design Principles

  1. One stage, one look. Each recognition stage has its own label and status style, used everywhere, so that a badge never looks like credit and a deposit never looks like a transfer ({{ir-stagecolours}}).
  2. Honest by default. Recognition text is generated, not typed; when the system cannot support a claim, it says what is true instead.
  3. Guide, then check. Forms explain the rule next to the field and check it as the user types, rather than failing at submission.
  4. Tasks first. Staff land on "what needs me today"; participants land on "my learning and my credit".
  5. Bilingual and mobile. Every participant task works on a phone in Bahasa Melayu or English.
  6. History is visible. Any record shows who decided what and when, one click away.

8.3 Status Vocabulary and Tag Policy

The statuses below appear across screens, credentials and notices. Colours use DESIGN.md semantic tokens, always with a text label (colour is never the only signal, {{nfr-wcag}}). In accordance with decision D-42, coloured background pills and badges are strictly reserved for operational states, eligibility conditions, and progress indicators (e.g. Passed (Final), 0 / 2 eligible, Provisional (1/3)). Decorative, technical-standard, or implementation tags (such as Ed25519, W3C VC 2.0, RFC 7519, or duplicate Course Completed badges beside completed headings) are prohibited from end-user screens.

8.4 Gaps in the Design System

DESIGN.md lists known gaps: it has no data tables, breadcrumbs, validation styling or dark theme, because it was extracted from marketing pages. MCMS is a data-heavy application, so the SDD shall define these components from Carbon's product guidance in the same flat, square style:

  • data tables with sorting, filtering, row selection and bulk actions (queues, cohort results, ledgers);
  • breadcrumbs and page headers for deep records (proposal → version → review);
  • form validation and error states (blocking errors versus warnings that need a response);
  • status tags for {{tab:statuses}}.

A dark theme is not needed for Release 1.
