1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Product Requirements Document (PRD) describes MCMS as a product: who it serves, what problems it solves, which features it offers, in which release each feature is delivered, and how success is measured. It complements the Software Requirements Specification (SRS). The SRS states precisely what the system shall do; this PRD explains what to build first, for whom and why, and turns the SRS into a release plan, user stories and a demonstrable Release 1 prototype (D-22).

Where this PRD and the SRS differ on a requirement, the SRS prevails; where this PRD and the Glossary and Decision Log differ on a term or decision, the Glossary and Decision Log prevails. Every feature and user story in this document cites the SRS requirements it delivers, so the two documents can be checked against each other.

2. Product Overview

2.1 Background and Problem Statement

UTeM offers three kinds of micro-credential (UMC, SAMC, MCE), each with different rules for SLT, quality assurance, credentials and credit. The Guide requires seven recognition stages — approval to offer, module achievement, MC completion, Quality Verification, ACR and ABC deposit, credit transfer and the award of a qualification — to be decided and recorded separately [G 12–14, 18.14]. Today these steps are handled with forms, spreadsheets and email, and the learning platform (UCreds) records learning but not academic recognition.

Problems MCMS addresses

2.2 Product Vision

Vision: Every UTeM micro-credential, from proposal to credit used in a degree, is managed in one trusted system where each recognition step is clear, honest and traceable — so that participants can trust what they earn, staff can follow the Guide without extra effort, and employers and programmes can verify what a credential really means.

2.3 Goals and Non-Goals

Product goals

Non-goals (things MCMS deliberately does not try to do):

  • Teach or host learning content — that stays on UCreds.
  • Decide academic matters — MCMS records decisions made by the authorised people; it never approves on their behalf.
  • Replace the national ABC platform, the QVC/MQA process, the student information system or the payment hub.
  • Promise credit, admission or a degree to participants (G 14.9).
