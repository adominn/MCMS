1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Design Description (SDD) describes how the UTeM Micro-Credential Management System (MCMS) is built: its architecture, technology, modules, data, interfaces, processes, security, quality attributes and deployment. Updated for Version 1.4 (next iteration, 11 November 2026), it reflects both the verified Release 1 Laravel implementation and the pilot/staging operational targets so developers, reviewers and CODL maintainers share one accurate specification.

The SRS states what the system shall do, the PRD what to build first and why, and the UI/UX Design Specification how it looks and behaves. This SDD states how it is made. Where this SDD and the SRS differ on a requirement, the SRS prevails. Where this SDD and the UI/UX Design Specification differ on a screen's look or behaviour, the UI/UX Design Specification prevails. Where they differ on a term or decision, the Glossary and Decision Log prevails.

1.2 Scope

The design covers the complete Release 1 system: every requirement of the SRS marked SHALL (Section 11.2 gives the module for each area and the cross-cutting table gives the rest), every Release 1 feature of the PRD, and every screen and document of the UI/UX Design Specification v1.5. It shows where Release 2 and 3 capabilities (SHOULD and MAY) will attach, without designing them in detail, so that they can be added without rework (D-24).

In this SDD "the prototype" or "Release 1 application" means the Laravel application in app/ designed and built here. The earlier clickable HTML prototype of the UI/UX Design Specification is called the design prototype (D-31, D-38).

MCMS is a new standalone web application with its own database, which reaches other systems only through adapters (D-06). Learning content stays on UCreds. The external Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) platform and the QVC/MQA procedures are outside the system; MCMS prepares and records, and UTeM staff submit (D-14).

1.7 Design Principles

The design applies the principles of the UI/UX Design Specification (Section 2.1) to the code:

  • One stage, one record. Each recognition stage is its own table, its own action and its own event (D-15). No action changes two stages. Verifying results and confirming completion are separate actions; the system issues credentials only from a completion confirmation (D-29).
  • Authority in the policy layer. Who may decide is enforced in policies on the server for every request, not only by hiding buttons (D-11, D-12).
  • Generated recognition text. Credit, MQF level and QV wording is computed from verified records and templates, never stored as free text (D-17).
  • Nothing is erased. Corrections add revisions and events; the audit trail is append-only (D-20).
  • Rules are settings. Thresholds and approving roles are versioned settings, and decisions record the version in force (D-16).
  • Adapters at the edge. Every external system can run as a stub, manual exchange or live connection without changing the modules (D-06, D-14).
