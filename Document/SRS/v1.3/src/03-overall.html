2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

MCMS is a new system. It does not extend UCreds or the student information system. It has its own database, sign-in handling and workflow, and it runs on UTeM-controlled servers (D-06, constraint C1, C2). MCMS is the system of record for micro-credential proposals, approvals, versions, offerings, enrolments, verified achievements, credentials, ACR preparation and the University's own credit-use records. It is not the system of record for learning content (UCreds), national credit storage (ABC), Quality Verification decisions (QVC/MQA) or programme admission and graduation (student information system); it stores references to those decisions.

MCMS talks to every external system through an adapter: a defined interface with a stub or manual implementation in Release 1 that can later be replaced by a live connector without changing the business modules (D-06, D-14). 2.2 Product Functions

MCMS will provide the following major functions. They are grouped into sixteen modules (M1–M16) carried from the draft SRS. The modules form a modular monolith: one deployable application with clearly separated modules that call each other through internal interfaces (D-07, proposed).

  1. Identity and governance foundation: sign-in, roles and scopes, settings, approving bodies, notifications and the integration hub.
  2. Curriculum management: category determination, proposal and design workspace, review and approval, versioning and material change.
  3. Offering and delivery: readiness checks, catalogue, registration and enrolment, connection to the learning platform, assessment and verification of achievement.
  4. Recognition: Achievement Record, Digital Badges, Certificates, Digital Credentials, public verification, and Quality Verification for SAMC.
  5. Credit: ACR, ABC deposit tracking, credit validity, credit transfer, single redemption and stackable degree pathways.
  6. Quality and records: monitoring, CQI, periodic review, suspension and termination, audit trail, records and reports.

2.3 The Central Rule: Separate Recognition Stages

The Guide treats each of the stages below as a different thing with its own decision and its own record, and it warns repeatedly against merging them [G 12–14, 15.4, 18.14]. MCMS SHALL store them as separate records with separate decisions, so that none can be skipped or mistaken for another (D-15). This is the single most important rule for developers. It overrides any convenience shortcut: no single status field, screen action or button may combine two stages.

2.5 User Classes, Characteristics, and Needs

MCMS has six kinds of user (D-28). The codes keep the numbering of Guide Appendix H2 so that requirements stay traceable to the Guide; the Guide's other general roles (a separate quality unit P4, records and credential unit P6, ABC depositor P7 and receiving-programme evaluator X4) are not used as separate users, and their tasks are carried by the deputy dean or done automatically by the system (D-28, D-29). A person may hold several roles, each with its own scope (Section 3.1.2).

2.6 Operating Environment

The platform will operate in the following environment:

  1. Technical environment
    • A web application accessible through current versions of Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox and Apple Safari (latest two major versions).
    • Fully responsive design for phones and tablets; no native mobile application.
    • Hosted on UTeM on-premises servers (virtual machines or containers), with separate development, test and production environments.
  2. Software environment
    • Laravel (PHP) application framework, a currently supported release (D-08).
    • PostgreSQL as the primary database; MySQL or MariaDB as an accepted fallback if CODL already hosts it (D-09, OI-02). Queries stay portable through Laravel's database layer.
    • A queue worker for background jobs (imports, credential issuance, notifications, alerts) and a scheduler for timed jobs (expiry alerts, retention checks).
    • UTeM single sign-on based on the Microsoft education account (OpenID Connect assumed, OI-03); UTeM SMTP email service.
  3. User environment
    • Staff use office desktops and laptops on the campus network or remotely over the internet.
    • Participants use personal devices, often phones, with varying connection quality.
    • Verifiers open a credential link or scan a QR code without signing in.
    • Interface in Bahasa Melayu and English.
  4. Organisational environment
    • Offices and data owners: PTj (academic owner of offerings and records), CODL (system operation), Pejabat Pendaftar, HEPA and UTeM ICT.

2.7 Design and Implementation Constraints

The following constraints will affect the design and implementation of the platform:

  1. Technical constraints
    • C1: MCMS SHALL be a new standalone system, not an extension of UCreds or the student information system (D-06).
    • C3: Every external system SHALL be reached through an adapter with a stub or manual implementation for Release 1 (D-06, D-14).
    • Architecture: a modular monolith built on Laravel (D-07, D-08).
    • C8: Credentials SHALL use an open, signed format; blockchain is not required (D-19).
  2. Data and rule constraints
    • C2: All data SHALL stay on UTeM-controlled infrastructure.
    • C4: Thresholds and lists SHALL be settings, not code (D-16).
    • C5: Credit value, MQF level and QV wording SHALL be generated only from verified records, never typed by hand (D-17).
    • C6: Approvals, credential actions, result changes and corrections SHALL be written to an append-only audit log (D-20).
    • The recognition stages SHALL be separate records (D-15).
  3. Regulatory constraints
    • Compliance with the Guide, MQA guidelines and the MQF.
    • Compliance with PDPA 2010 and UTeM policies on records, information security and privacy.
  4. Business constraints
    • Built by an intern as a concept and prototype; Release 1 is the prototype scope (D-22).
    • Maintainable by CODL staff who mainly use PHP (D-08).
    • Approving bodies and role holders may change; the system must adapt by configuration (D-11, D-12).
  5. User constraints
    • C7: Interface and credentials SHALL support Bahasa Melayu and English (D-02).
    • Accessible to users with disabilities (WCAG 2.1 AA) and usable on low-end phones.

2.8 User Documentation

The following user documentation will be developed with the system:

  1. Online help: context help on each screen and a Frequently Asked Questions page that explains, in plain words, the difference between a badge, a certificate, an ACR, an ABC deposit and a credit transfer.
  2. User guides, in Bahasa Melayu and English: Lecturer and Developer Guide; PTj and Approver Guide; Quality Assurance Guide; Records, Credential and ABC Guide; Participant Guide; System Administrator Guide.
  3. Technical documentation: adapter interface guide, CSV import and export templates, credential format and verification guide, installation and operations guide.
  4. Training materials: quick reference cards per role and short demonstration videos.

2.9 Assumptions and Dependencies

Assumptions

The assumptions below are carried from the Glossary and Decision Log. Each one is built so that, if it proves wrong, only configuration or a single adapter changes.

Dependencies

  1. Access to the UTeM SSO tenant and agreement on the sign-in protocol (OI-03).
  2. Agreement with the UCreds team on the data exchange format (OI-04).
  3. Availability of a database server and hosting environment at UTeM (OI-02).
  4. Reference data for PTj, programmes and parent courses, including accreditation status, from the relevant offices.
  5. Decisions on the approving bodies, retention periods and ownership of credential signing keys (OI-06, OI-07, OI-08).
