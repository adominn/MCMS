1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) gives a complete description of the UTeM Micro-Credential Management System (MCMS). MCMS is a proposed system that manages the whole life of a UTeM micro-credential: proposal and approval, offering and enrolment, learning results and credentials, Academic Credit Record (ACR) and Academic Bank of Credit (ABC) tracking, credit transfer, stackable degree pathways, and monitoring with Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI).

The document states what the system shall do, for whom, and under which rules. It does not state how the system is built; code structure, screen layouts and database scripts belong to the Software Design Description (SDD). The SRS is the agreed basis for the PRD, the SDD, development, and acceptance testing. Every requirement has a stable identifier so that it can be traced back to the Guide clause it comes from and forward to the design and the tests that prove it (decision D-01).

The document is written for two readers at once: a supervisor who reviews it, and developers and AI development agents who use it as the rule set for building the system (D-05). For that reason each requirement is a single statement, the source of every business rule is cited, and every assumption is visible.

1.4 Project Scope

UTeM offers micro-credentials in three categories: Unbundled Micro-Credentials (UMC) taken from accredited credit courses, Stand-alone Micro-Credentials (SAMC), and the non-credit Micro-Credential Eksekutif (MCE). The Garis Panduan Micro-Credential UTeM sets strict rules for each category. Most importantly, it separates approval, achievement, credentials, Quality Verification, ACR and ABC deposit, credit transfer and the award of a qualification into distinct steps that must never be merged [G 12–14, 18.14]. These rules are difficult to apply consistently with documents and spreadsheets. MCMS gives the University one system in which each step is a separate, traceable record.

MCMS is a new, standalone web system with its own database, sign-in handling and workflow (D-06). Learning content stays on the UCreds platform, and other external services are reached through adapters.

In Scope

The system will include the following key components:

  • Identity, roles and settings: single sign-on with the Microsoft education account, role- and scope-based access, configurable thresholds and approving bodies.
  • Curriculum management: category determination for UMC, SAMC and MCE, the proposal and design workspace of Guide Appendix A, review, approval, versioning and material-change control.
  • Offering and delivery: readiness checks (Appendix B), public catalogue, registration, enrolment, cohorts, a dummy payment service, and import of learning results from UCreds.
  • Achievement and credentials: assessment verification, the internal Achievement Record, Digital Badges, Certificates and Digital Credentials in an open signed format, and public verification without login.
  • Quality Verification for SAMC: QV readiness evidence (Appendix D), QV status, validity and expiry control.
  • Credit recognition: ACR preparation, participant consent, ABC deposit tracking, credit validity, credit transfer evaluation, the Single Redemption Principle and stackable degree pathway checks (Appendices F and G).
  • Governance: monitoring, CQI (Appendix C), periodic review, suspension and termination, the append-only audit trail, records management and reports (Appendix H).
  • Bilingual operation: screens, notifications and credentials in Bahasa Melayu and English (D-02).

Out of Scope

The following items are explicitly excluded:

  1. Delivering learning content; this stays on UCreds or another learning platform.
  2. The national ABC platform and the QVC/MQA procedures themselves. MCMS prepares and tracks; UTeM staff submit (D-14).
  3. The full student information system, programme admission and degree programme regulations. MCMS records the results of these decisions but does not take them.
  4. The UTeM payment hub itself; a dummy payment service stands in for Release 1 (A-03).
  5. Open public self-registration; outside applicants first register at Pejabat Pendaftar or HEPA (D-10).
  6. Partner-delivered micro-credentials run by other institutions (possible later).
  7. Hardware procurement, and native mobile applications.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

MCMS is a new system. It does not extend UCreds or the student information system. It has its own database, sign-in handling and workflow, and it runs on UTeM-controlled servers (D-06, constraint C1, C2). MCMS is the system of record for micro-credential proposals, approvals, versions, offerings, enrolments, verified achievements, credentials, ACR preparation and the University's own credit-use records. It is not the system of record for learning content (UCreds), national credit storage (ABC), Quality Verification decisions (QVC/MQA) or programme admission and graduation (student information system); it stores references to those decisions.

MCMS talks to every external system through an adapter: a defined interface with a stub or manual implementation in Release 1 that can later be replaced by a live connector without changing the business modules (D-06, D-14). The context diagram below shows who uses MCMS and which systems it connects to.

2.2 Product Functions

MCMS will provide the following major functions. They are grouped into sixteen modules (M1–M16) carried from the draft SRS. The modules form a modular monolith: one deployable application with clearly separated modules that call each other through internal interfaces (D-07, proposed).

  1. Identity and governance foundation: sign-in, roles and scopes, settings, approving bodies, notifications and the integration hub.
  2. Curriculum management: category determination, proposal and design workspace, review and approval, versioning and material change.
  3. Offering and delivery: readiness checks, catalogue, registration and enrolment, connection to the learning platform, assessment and verification of achievement.
  4. Recognition: Achievement Record, Digital Badges, Certificates, Digital Credentials, public verification, and Quality Verification for SAMC.
  5. Credit: ACR, ABC deposit tracking, credit validity, credit transfer, single redemption and stackable degree pathways.
  6. Quality and records: monitoring, CQI, periodic review, suspension and termination, audit trail, records and reports.

Modules of MCMS

2.3 The Central Rule: Separate Recognition Stages

The Guide treats each of the stages below as a different thing with its own decision and its own record, and it warns repeatedly against merging them [G 12–14, 15.4, 18.14]. MCMS SHALL store them as separate records with separate decisions, so that none can be skipped or mistaken for another (D-15). This is the single most important rule for developers. It overrides any convenience shortcut: no single status field, screen action or button may combine two stages.

2.6 Operating Environment

The platform will operate in the following environment:

  1. Technical environment
    • A web application accessible through current versions of Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox and Apple Safari (latest two major versions).
    • Fully responsive design for phones and tablets; no native mobile application.
    • Hosted on UTeM on-premises servers (virtual machines or containers), with separate development, test and production environments.
  2. Software environment
    • Laravel (PHP) application framework, a currently supported release (D-08).
    • PostgreSQL as the primary database; MySQL or MariaDB as an accepted fallback if CODL already hosts it (D-09, OI-02). Queries stay portable through Laravel's database layer.
    • A queue worker for background jobs (imports, credential issuance, notifications, alerts) and a scheduler for timed jobs (expiry alerts, retention checks).
    • UTeM single sign-on based on the Microsoft education account (OpenID Connect assumed, OI-03); UTeM SMTP email service.
  3. User environment
    • Staff use office desktops and laptops on the campus network or remotely over the internet.
    • Participants use personal devices, often phones, with varying connection quality.
    • Verifiers open a credential link or scan a QR code without signing in.
    • Interface in Bahasa Melayu and English.
  4. Organisational environment
    • Offices and data owners: PTj (academic owner of offerings and records), CODL (system operation), Pejabat Pendaftar, HEPA and UTeM ICT.

2.7 Design and Implementation Constraints

The following constraints will affect the design and implementation of the platform:

  1. Technical constraints
    • C1: MCMS SHALL be a new standalone system, not an extension of UCreds or the student information system (D-06).
    • C3: Every external system SHALL be reached through an adapter with a stub or manual implementation for Release 1 (D-06, D-14).
    • Architecture: a modular monolith built on Laravel (D-07, D-08).
    • C8: Credentials SHALL use an open, signed format; blockchain is not required (D-19).
  2. Data and rule constraints
    • C2: All data SHALL stay on UTeM-controlled infrastructure.
    • C4: Thresholds and lists SHALL be settings, not code (D-16).
    • C5: Credit value, MQF level and QV wording SHALL be generated only from verified records, never typed by hand (D-17).
    • C6: Approvals, credential actions, result changes and corrections SHALL be written to an append-only audit log (D-20).
    • The recognition stages SHALL be separate records (D-15).
  3. Regulatory constraints
    • Compliance with the Guide, MQA guidelines and the MQF.
    • Compliance with PDPA 2010 and UTeM policies on records, information security and privacy.
  4. Business constraints
    • Built by an intern as a concept and prototype; Release 1 is the prototype scope (D-22).
    • Maintainable by CODL staff who mainly use PHP (D-08).
    • Approving bodies and role holders may change; the system must adapt by configuration (D-11, D-12).
  5. User constraints
    • C7: Interface and credentials SHALL support Bahasa Melayu and English (D-02).
    • Accessible to users with disabilities (WCAG 2.1 AA) and usable on low-end phones.

2.8 User Documentation

The following user documentation will be developed with the system:

  1. Online help: context help on each screen and a Frequently Asked Questions page that explains, in plain words, the difference between a badge, a certificate, an ACR, an ABC deposit and a credit transfer.
  2. User guides, in Bahasa Melayu and English: Lecturer and Developer Guide; PTj and Approver Guide; Quality Assurance Guide; Records, Credential and ABC Guide; Participant Guide; System Administrator Guide.
  3. Technical documentation: adapter interface guide, CSV import and export templates, credential format and verification guide, installation and operations guide.
  4. Training materials: quick reference cards per role and short demonstration videos.

3.5 Registration, Enrolment and Cohorts (M6)

Participants register for open offerings after reading the offering information. Fees are handled through the payment adapter; in Release 1 a dummy payment service records simulated payments (A-03). For a modular UMC, participants may complete modules in different periods, and each module status is kept separately [G 10.3, 12.2].

3.6 Learning Platform Connection (M7)

Learning takes place on UCreds, the pilot learning platform. MCMS exchanges enrolments, progress, module completion, results and badge data with UCreds through the learning platform adapter. Because the connection method is not yet known, Release 1 uses CSV files (D-13, A-04, OI-04). Imported data is never final: it lands as provisional data awaiting verification (Section 3.7).

3.7 Assessment and Achievement Verification (M8)

Recognition follows achievement, not attendance [G 6.1, 8.6]. Results are entered or imported, then verified by the owning PTj before any badge, certificate or Digital Credential is issued. Verification links the participant's identity, the assessment evidence and the result [G 10.4].

3.8 Achievement Record (M9)

The Achievement Record (Rekod Pencapaian Pembelajaran Micro-Credential UTeM) is UTeM's internal record of modules completed, results, badges and MC completion. It is not an ACR and it is not evidence that credit was deposited to ABC or accepted by a programme [G 8.7.5, 18.3]. {{fig:umcflow}} shows how a modular UMC builds up to completion.

3.9 Credential Issuance and Verification (M10)

3.10 Quality Verification Management for SAMC (M11)

Quality Verification (QV) is the quality check for a SAMC carried out by the QVC under MQA rules. It is separate from UTeM's approval to offer: approval lets a micro-credential be offered; QV confirms its quality and gives it a credit value and MQF level [G 11.2]. QV requires at least one completed cohort and is valid for at most three continuous years [G 11.2.1–11.2.2]. In Release 1, MCMS prepares and tracks; UTeM staff submit to QVC/MQA outside the system (D-14).

3.11 ACR and ABC Deposit (M12)

For eligible learning, an Academic Credit Record (ACR) can be prepared and deposited to the Academic Bank of Credit (ABC). Only two sources are eligible: a completed UMC from an accredited programme (formal learning) and a completed Quality-Verified SAMC (non-formal learning) [G 12.1.1]. The deposit is storage, not approval: credit in ABC still has to be evaluated by a receiving programme before it can be used [G 12.5]. The participant's consent is required [G 12.4.2]. {{fig:acrseq}} shows the Release 1 process, in which the system generates the ACR, the deputy dean confirms it and, after the participant consents, records the deposit; the national ABC platform is still a concept, so no live connection exists yet (D-14, D-29).

3.12 Credit Transfer (M13)

Credit transfer is the evaluation and acceptance, by the programme that receives it (in MCMS, the deputy dean of the faculty that owns the degree, D-11), of valid credit to meet part of its requirements [G 13]. It is separate from accumulation: credit that is verified, recorded in an ACR or deposited in ABC must still be evaluated. Equivalence is judged on learning outcomes and related criteria, never on credit value or SLT alone [G 13.3].

3.13 Single Redemption of Credit (M12)

Credit that has been used toward one qualification cannot be used toward another. Transferred credit is provisionally redeemed until the participant completes the programme; it then becomes redeemed. If the participant does not complete the programme, the credit can become available again while it is still valid [G 12.9].

3.14 Stackable Degree Pathway (M13)

A stackable degree pathway lets eligible credit from micro-credentials count toward part of a degree. It is not a sum of credits. The participant must be admitted to the programme, have credit approved through transfer, meet the minimum of 30% of graduating credit earned at UTeM, meet residency for conventional programmes, cover the programme learning outcomes and Body of Knowledge, and meet all graduation requirements [G 14]. MCMS supports two levels: a pathway mapping for a target programme, approved by the academic authority, and an individual stackable plan per participant, using Guide Appendix G.
