1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This UI/UX Design Specification describes how MCMS looks and behaves for each of its users: the visual system, the interaction rules, every screen, every flow between screens and roles, and every printable document. It turns the requirements of the SRS and the product decisions of the PRD into a design that developers can build without guessing.

The specification was written from the approved clickable prototype v2. The prototype is part of the specification: where a screen is described here, the prototype shows it with sample data, and where this text and the prototype differ, this text prevails and the prototype is corrected. Where this document and the SRS differ on a requirement, the SRS prevails.

1.2 Document Conventions

Note: Names, records and figures in the screenshots are sample data of the prototype. They are consistent with each other but are not real people or decisions.

1.3 Intended Audience

1.4 Scope

The specification covers the Release 1 web application for all roles: participants (students), lecturers and colleague reviewers, deputy deans, the CODL system administrator and the industry expert. It also covers the public website for visitors and employers, and the documents the system prints or exports. The application is web first and responsive down to a phone width of 360 px (NFR responsiveness); there is no native mobile app (SRS 1.4).

Out of scope: the visual design of UCreds, the national ABC platform and other external systems; the SDD's technical design of components; and Release 2 features beyond showing them as disabled.

2. Design Principles and Users

2.1 Design Principles

Seven principles drive every design decision. They come from the Guide's separation of recognition stages, the PRD design principles (PRD 8.2) and the user needs of the personas.

2.2 Users and Permissions

The design follows the six kinds of user of decision D-28. {{tab:perm}} shows what each role can and cannot do in the interface; buttons for actions a role cannot take are not shown at all, rather than shown disabled.

Roles and their permissions in the interface

A person may hold several roles, each with its own scope. Separation of duties is checked per record: a lecturer who is also a reviewer cannot review their own proposal, and a person who entered a result cannot be its only verifier.

2.3 Personas and Sample Data

The personas of PRD Section 3 appear in the prototype with consistent sample data. The world is set on 26 October 2026 at FTMK, with records at different stages so that every flow can be walked: the UMC Data Structures (BITS 1123, 3 credits, 120 hours) with cohorts from 2025/2 to 2027/1, the SAMC Cloud Native Development (2 credits, MQF level 4, Quality-Verified to 6 December 2026), the MCE Leadership Essentials (16 hours, no credit), and Amir's two deposited or pending credits. Figures add up across screens, for example the 28 learners of Data Structures 2026/2, of whom 27 pass.

3. Visual Design System

MCMS uses the design system of DESIGN.md, "ulearn-carbon": IBM Carbon geometry and type with uLearn Navy as the single brand colour. This section fixes how that system is applied to working screens. {{fig:shell}} shows the result on the deputy dean's task queue.

3.1 Colour

Colour roles

Note: The navy side navigation is the owner's decision of 26 October 2026, overriding the neutral-sidebar guidance of the design craft rules, to give the uLearn brand colour more presence. Content areas stay white.

3.2 Typography

IBM Plex Sans is the only typeface; IBM Plex Mono is used only for identifiers such as setting keys and file names. Every number in a table, KPI or form is set in tabular figures and right-aligned.

3.3 Shape, Spacing and Elevation

Corners are square (0 px) for buttons, inputs, panels and modals; only status tags are pill-shaped. Spacing follows a 4 px grid: 24 px between sections, 16 px between fields, 20 px panel padding. Panels are separated by 1 px hairlines and surface change, never by shadows. Shadows are reserved for what floats: menus, toasts, dialogs and drawers.

4. Interaction Design

4.1 Every Control Has a Defined Result

Each button, link, tab, table row and menu item does exactly one of the things in {{tab:results}}. A control that does nothing is a defect. A control that cannot be used yet is shown disabled with the reason in its label or beside it.

Possible results of a control

4.2 Confirmation Dialogs

The system asks for confirmation, with a summary of the effect, before: submit, endorse, return, record a decision, verify results, confirm completion (which issues credentials), correct, replace, revoke, confirm ACR, record a deposit, decide a transfer, redeem or release credit, change a setting, assign or remove a role, deactivate an account, import a file, withdraw an enrolment, give or withdraw consent, pay, and sign out with unsaved work (ir-confirm).

4.3 Feedback

4.4 Notifications and Profile

The bell in the top bar shows the unread count and opens a panel with the five latest notifications, "Mark all read" and "View all". The notifications page filters by read state, marks a notification read when it is opened and takes the user to the record. Decisions on the user's records, credential actions, consent requests and enrolment changes are mandatory notices that cannot be switched off (fr-ntprefs). Lecturers and deputy deans may choose a daily digest instead of single emails.

The profile button opens a menu: My profile, Notification preferences, Sign-in and sessions, Help and guides, and Sign out (which asks for confirmation). The profile page has four tabs: Details (identity from SSO, read-only; language, phone and name on certificates editable), Notifications, Sign-in and sessions (MFA, active sessions, idle timeout) and My personal data (view and request a correction, sr-access).

4.5 Tables and Pagination

Every list that can exceed one page is paginated: rows per page (5, 10, 25 or 50; default 10), a count "1–10 of 28", "Page 1 of 3" and previous and next buttons. Long lists have a search box above the table and filters as selects in the same bar; counts follow the filter. A row that opens a record is clickable across the whole row. A bulk action is one filled button above the table that names the count. Rows that need the user's attention, such as missing marks, are listed first.

4.6 Session, Sign-in and Maintenance

Staff sign in with the UTeM Microsoft account and a second step (MFA); participants sign in without the second step. There is no MCMS password and no public sign-up; an account that is not recognised gets a page explaining how external learners register at Pejabat Pendaftar or HEPA (D-10, A-06). Two minutes before the 30-minute idle timeout a dialog offers "Stay signed in" or "Sign out now". Planned maintenance is announced in a banner 48 hours ahead.

4.7 Accessibility and Language

  • Every control works by keyboard: links, tabs, table rows and menu items are focusable, and Enter or Space activates them. Escape closes dialogs, drawers and menus; focus stays inside an open dialog and returns to the control that opened it.
  • Every field has a visible label; placeholders never replace labels. Status is never shown by colour alone; every tag carries its word.
  • Text meets 4.5:1 contrast; the navy navigation uses white and light-blue text that meets it on #1e2a6e.
  • Every screen, notification and credential exists in BM and EN. The language switch changes the language in place and keeps what the user has typed (nfr-langswitch). Bilingual content fields mark a missing language.
  • The layout reflows to one column below 760 px; the side navigation becomes a scrollable strip, and tables scroll inside their frame (ir-responsive).

4.8 Status Vocabulary

Statuses use the exact words of PRD 8.3 and the SRS state diagrams. Each status has a short explanation where it first appears on a screen (nfr-plain). Negative statuses are checked before positive ones, so "Not approved" is never shown as an approval.

Status words by record type

4.9 Formats

Dates are shown as DD/MM/YYYY and times as 24-hour Malaysia time (ir-dates, ir-time). Money is shown as "RM 450.00" (nfr-currency). Names are printed in full, including bin, binti, a/l and a/p (nfr-names). Setting keys and file names use the mono face.

5. Navigation

Each role has its own side navigation, grouped by the kind of work, with its home page first. Counts beside an item come from the live task data and fall as work is done. The profile menu, notifications, help and search are the same for every role; search results always stay inside the signed-in role's own screens, so a search never switches role.

Side navigation by role

Links that lead from one role's screen into another role's work are not offered: for example, CODL is told which marks are missing but cannot open the lecturer's marks screen, and the deputy dean's traceability view does not open CODL's audit screen. Breadcrumbs show the path inside a role.

6.4 Public Website

The public site informs before it sells. The home page explains the three categories, features the next open offerings, explains how recognition works and links to verification. Offering pages follow the structure of the UCreds course pages: a facts block (institution, format, effort per week, dates, duration, cost, credit, places) and tabs for Overview, Syllabus, Assessment, Prerequisites, Recognition and FAQ, with an Enrol button. Credit is shown only when it is verified for the cohort's dates; the separate-stages notice states that completion, ACR, deposit and transfer are separate with no guarantee.

6.5 CODL Administrator

CODL lands on users and roles with a "Needs you" panel (failing adapter, templates missing a language, disposals waiting for the Registrar, imports with problems). Role assignment requires a scope, a reason and an effective date; a future date is scheduled; an administrator cannot change their own roles or remove the only deputy dean of a faculty. Settings changes need a reason and a future effective date and keep a history; the public verification fields can only be chosen from the five allowed fields. Templates cannot be published when the BM and EN placeholders differ or when the text makes an unsupported claim such as an automatic transfer. UCreds imports stage rows as Provisional and never create achievements. No CODL screen has an academic decision; where one might be expected, a note names who decides.

7. User Flows

A flow is a process walked across screens and, often, across roles. Each flow of {{tab:flows}} is in the prototype's Flows page; starting one shows a step bar at the bottom of the screen that says what is happening and what to click next. Negative paths have flows of their own, for example a payment failure, a transfer line that cannot be approved, or a credential that was revoked.

7.1 End-to-End Journey

{{fig:e2e}} shows the journey of SRS use case A.5 as it runs in the prototype (flow FL-19). Each box is a separate decision by a separate role; the system issues credentials and generates the ACR only after the deputy dean's decision.

7.2 Hand-offs Between Roles

When one role finishes a step, the next role sees it at once: a task appears in the deputy dean's queue, a notification reaches the person affected, and the record's status and history change. {{tab:handoffs}} lists the hand-offs the design guarantees; each is tested end to end in the prototype.

8. Printable Documents and Content

8.1 Printable Documents

Every download, print or export in MCMS opens a document laid out on A4 pages, which the user can print or save as PDF. Where a machine-readable file is needed, the document also offers a real download. Each page has a header with the UTeM name, the document title, reference and date, and a footer with the reference, page number, generation time and "Generated by MCMS". Documents for participants are bilingual, with BM beneath EN (D-02). No identity number, email or marks appear on documents that leave UTeM.

9. Traceability and Verification

9.1 Requirements to Screens

{{tab:trace}} maps each SRS functional module to the screens that deliver it. The per-requirement detail (actor, action, confirmation, result and blocking checks) is kept in UIUX/v2/basis/srs-fr-inventory.md.

9.2 How the Design Was Verified

The prototype was checked in three ways before approval:

  1. Automated screen checks. Every screen draws without script errors and every link, dialog, drawer and action it contains exists (the 114 screens plus the prototype's two index pages, 51 dialogs and panels, 0 problems).
  2. Hand-off test. A scripted walk through the real screens checks the role hand-offs of Section 7.2 (14 checks, all passing).
  3. Design review. Three independent reviews compared every screen with the design basis and the SRS. They found 124 issues: 23 that broke a rule or blocked a flow, 70 missing states or confusing behaviours, and 31 polish items. All rule and flow issues were fixed.

10.3 Prototype Limits

  • The prototype runs in a browser with sample data kept for the browser tab; "Reset data" restores the starting state. It has no server, accounts or real integrations.

Lecturer · 5 steps

Step 4 of 5. Click Submit for review, write a short response to the warning, and confirm. The status becomes Submitted and the content locks. (#/lec/proposal/ST?tab=mods)
Step 5 of 5. Hakim Rahman now has a task "Assign reviewers" for Software Testing. (#/dd/queue?area=proposals)

D.4 FL-03 Review a colleague's proposal

Lecturer as reviewer · 4 steps

Step 1 of 4. Dr. Aina has an invitation to review Cyber Security Operations Fundamentals, due 30 Oct. (#/lec/reviews)
Step 2 of 4. Step 1: declare a conflict of interest. The proposal stays closed until you do. Choose "I have no conflict of interest" and confirm. Choosing a conflict declines the review instead. (#/lec/review/RV-031)
Step 3 of 4. Step 2: rate each item Satisfactory or Needs amendment. A Needs amendment rating needs a comment and can be marked blocking. (#/lec/review/RV-031)
Step 4 of 4. Step 3: choose Support, Amend or Do not support, add a summary, and submit. Submit stays disabled until everything is filled. (#/lec/review/RV-031)
