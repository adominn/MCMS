1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Product Requirements Document (PRD) describes MCMS as a product: who it serves, what problems it solves, which features it offers, in which release each feature is delivered, and how success is measured. It complements the Software Requirements Specification (SRS). The SRS states precisely what the system shall do; this PRD explains what to build first, for whom and why, and turns the SRS into a release plan, user stories and a demonstrable Release 1 prototype (D-22).

Where this PRD and the SRS differ on a requirement, the SRS prevails; where this PRD and the Glossary and Decision Log differ on a term or decision, the Glossary and Decision Log prevails. Every feature and user story in this document cites the SRS requirements it delivers, so the two documents can be checked against each other.

2. Product Overview

2.1 Background and Problem Statement

UTeM offers three kinds of micro-credential (UMC, SAMC, MCE), each with different rules for SLT, quality assurance, credentials and credit. The Guide requires seven recognition stages — approval to offer, module achievement, MC completion, Quality Verification, ACR and ABC deposit, credit transfer and the award of a qualification — to be decided and recorded separately [G 12–14, 18.14]. Today these steps are handled with forms, spreadsheets and email, and the learning platform (UCreds) records learning but not academic recognition.

Problems MCMS addresses

2.2 Product Vision

Vision: Every UTeM micro-credential, from proposal to credit used in a degree, is managed in one trusted system where each recognition step is clear, honest and traceable — so that participants can trust what they earn, staff can follow the Guide without extra effort, and employers and programmes can verify what a credential really means.

2.3 Goals and Non-Goals

Product goals

Non-goals (things MCMS deliberately does not try to do):

  • Teach or host learning content — that stays on UCreds.
  • Decide academic matters — MCMS records decisions made by the authorised people; it never approves on their behalf.
  • Replace the national ABC platform, the QVC/MQA process, the student information system or the payment hub.
  • Promise credit, admission or a degree to participants (G 14.9).

Journeys show the experience of a persona across several features. The target experience rates each step with MCMS on a scale of 1 (frustrating) to 5 (effortless). Steps that stay at 3 are limited by processes outside MCMS, such as committee meetings, which MCMS records but does not control. Each journey crosses several recognition stages; the stage column shows that the participant or officer always knows which stage they are in.

4.3 Deputy Dean: Closing a Cohort

Hakim closes a cohort: results, credentials, ACR and deposit

4.4 Employer: Verifying a Credential

Tan opens the link or scans the QR code on a certificate. The verification page loads without login and shows the credential name, the holder's name, UTeM as issuer, the issue date and the status (valid, corrected, replaced or revoked). Nothing else about the holder is shown ({{fr-vffields}}). If the credential was revoked, the page says so with the date. The whole journey takes under a minute and needs no contact with UTeM.

5.5 Definition of Done for Release 1

Release 1 is done when all of the following hold. They extend the acceptance approach of SRS Section 8.3.

  1. Every user story of Section 6 marked R1 meets its acceptance criteria in the test environment.
  2. Every business rule BR-01 to {{br-authority}} has an automated test that proves the forbidden case is refused ({{nfr-rulestests}}).
  3. The demonstration scenario of Section 7 runs end to end on seed data with stub adapters.
  4. The interface works in Bahasa Melayu and English and passes an automated WCAG 2.1 AA check on the main participant screens.
  5. The SDD describes the system as built.

5.6 Out of Scope for This Product Version

  • Delivering learning content, live classes or proctoring (UCreds and other tools).
  • Taking academic decisions automatically; MCMS proposes, checks and records, people decide.
  • Native mobile applications; the web interface is responsive instead.
  • Partner-delivered micro-credentials of other institutions as offerings in MCMS.
  • Refund processing and financial accounting.

8. Experience and Design Principles

8.1 Design System

All screens follow the project design system DESIGN.md "ulearn-carbon": IBM Carbon's flat, square geometry; IBM Plex Sans; white and light-gray surfaces; charcoal text; and uLearn Navy (#1e2a6e) as the single accent for links, primary actions and focus. MCMS adds the product rules below on top of it.

8.2 Product Design Principles

  1. One stage, one look. Each recognition stage has its own label and status style, used everywhere, so that a badge never looks like credit and a deposit never looks like a transfer ({{ir-stagecolours}}).
  2. Honest by default. Recognition text is generated, not typed; when the system cannot support a claim, it says what is true instead.
  3. Guide, then check. Forms explain the rule next to the field and check it as the user types, rather than failing at submission.
  4. Tasks first. Staff land on "what needs me today"; participants land on "my learning and my credit".
  5. Bilingual and mobile. Every participant task works on a phone in Bahasa Melayu or English.
  6. History is visible. Any record shows who decided what and when, one click away.

8.3 Status Vocabulary

The statuses below appear across screens, credentials and notices. Colours use DESIGN.md semantic tokens, always with a text label (colour is never the only signal, {{nfr-wcag}}).

Status vocabulary and visual treatment

8.4 Gaps in the Design System

DESIGN.md lists known gaps: it has no data tables, breadcrumbs, validation styling or dark theme, because it was extracted from marketing pages. MCMS is a data-heavy application, so the SDD shall define these components from Carbon's product guidance in the same flat, square style:

  • data tables with sorting, filtering, row selection and bulk actions (queues, cohort results, ledgers);
  • breadcrumbs and page headers for deep records (proposal → version → review);
  • form validation and error states (blocking errors versus warnings that need a response);
  • status tags for {{tab:statuses}}.

A dark theme is not needed for Release 1.
